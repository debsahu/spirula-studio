Spirula Studio's densify stage takes design ideas from this pull request. It
does not merge, cherry-pick or apply any of its commits. Each idea below was
re-implemented on the roma::Matcher seam (src/roma/Matcher.h) with its own
tests; nothing in src/nn/ comes from the pull request. Both trees are GPLv3
(LICENSE at the repository root).

Bidirectional cycle check
  Taken as a design:   rejecting a match whose reverse prediction does not
                       return to the source pixel, the threshold in matcher
                       cells, applied only when B -> A exists
                       (src/dense/DenseConfig.h max_cycle_error / cycle_check,
                       src/dense/Reconstruction.cpp direction()).
  Written here:        roma::cycleError and the per-neighbour filter in
                       src/roma/Densify.cpp, the B -> A plumbing (matchBoth or a
                       second match) in src/roma/DensifyRun.cpp, --cycle in
                       src/app/cli/densify_main.cpp, tests in
                       src/roma/tests/roma_densify_test.cpp. Our threshold comes
                       from a measured distribution (--cycle measure), not
                       their default of 1; a missing reverse refuses instead of
                       passing; the reverse's certainty is not read.

Precision-weighted robust point refinement
  Taken as a design:   refining a fused point with RoMa's 2x2 precision as the
                       residual's information, under a Huber loss by
                       iteratively reweighted Gauss-Newton, one observation per
                       source image (src/dense/Geometry.cpp optimize(),
                       refine_point()).
  Written here:        refineAlongRay and the refinement pass in
                       src/roma/Densify.cpp; Warp::precision on the seam
                       (src/roma/Matcher.h), filled from the model's own
                       confidence in src/roma/model/RomaMatcher.cpp; .rwm
                       encoding 2 in src/roma/DumpMatcher.cpp. Ours is
                       one-dimensional along the reference ray (the reference
                       pixel is where the warp is defined, so it carries no
                       error), its Huber threshold is in sigmas of each view's
                       own residuals rather than in pixels, weights are the
                       precision alone (not times the overlap), and it runs
                       after our largest-consistent-set fusion, before every
                       per-point filter, which then judge the refined point.
